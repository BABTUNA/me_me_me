I fumbled the bag at places like **Oracle**, **Bloomberg**, and **Palantir Technologies** because I got cooked on **system design**.

And the reality is, learning system design is not even that hard once you grasp the basics. The problem is that most learning materials out there suck at showing the **logical progression** of why certain architectures get introduced as systems scale with more users.

Reading through dozens of disconnected articles to piece that story together was not fun, to say the least.

So I built **[Lattis](https://www.lattissystems.design/)**: a way to visualize how architecture decisions are made, step by step, as load grows.

## The gap I kept hitting

System design interviews are not really asking you to draw boxes. They are asking whether you understand **why** each box appears in a specific order:

- When do you add a cache, and what breaks if you add it too early?
- Why does fan-out show up before you ever talk about sharding?
- What changes when one celebrity user dwarfs everyone else on the timeline?

Most prep content gives you a finished diagram and a wall of text. You memorize the picture, but the **sequence of decisions** does not stick. That is exactly where I kept losing rounds.

## What Lattis does

[Lattis](https://www.lattissystems.design/) is an interactive system design curriculum with **3D walkthroughs** that build as you read. Click a step, watch the cache appear, see traffic move across the system.

The idea is simple:

1. **One small idea per step.** Twelve to fourteen steps per lesson. Each step adds one decision, not a lecture before anything renders.
2. **A scene that moves with the words.** No static boxes-and-arrows screenshots. The diagram updates when the concept shows up in the narrative.
3. **Tradeoffs next to the move.** Reasons stay attached to the decision that triggered them, so the ordering of choices stays intact.

You leave with a way of thinking, not a slide to memorize.
